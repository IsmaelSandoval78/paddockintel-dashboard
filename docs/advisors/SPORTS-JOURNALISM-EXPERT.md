# PaddockIntel — Sports Journalism Expert (.md advisor)

Read this before publishing any article, digest issue, or newsletter copy — alongside
EDITORIAL.md, which this advisor does not replace or duplicate. EDITORIAL.md is the voice and
structure bible (The Line, the five-section structure, the frontmatter). This advisor is the
journalism-craft layer on top: is this actually good sports journalism, independent of whether
it follows the template correctly.

---

## The core test (from EDITORIAL.md, restated as the working filter)

**Could this line appear in Bloomberg Businessweek's sports section?**
If yes → publish. If it sounds like a fan forum post → rewrite the angle. This is the single
fastest gut-check before anything else on this list.

## Reporting discipline

- [ ] Distinguish reported fact from analysis from opinion, visibly — a reader should never
      have to guess which sentence is verified fact vs. PaddockIntel's read on it. The
      Verdict section is where opinion is allowed and labeled as such (first person permitted
      there specifically) — nowhere else in the five-section structure
- [ ] Never bury the actual news under scene-setting — "What Happened" answers who/what/
      when/where in the first paragraph, not the fifth
- [ ] Quotes are real, sourced, and never edited to change meaning — trimming for length is
      fine, altering intent is not
- [ ] If a story involves a developing situation (injury, contract dispute, team conflict),
      state clearly what is confirmed vs. what is reported-but-unconfirmed — do not present
      paddock rumor with the same certainty as an official statement

## Angle quality (the actual differentiation test)

- [ ] The "angle sentence" exists and is sharp: *"This article argues that [X] because [Y]."*
      If this sentence can't be written cleanly, the piece isn't ready to draft
- [ ] The angle is genuinely economic/data-driven, not drama re-labeled with a business word
      stapled on — "Alpine loses another engineer" framed as "brain drain crisis!!" with a
      dollar sign added is not the PaddockIntel angle, it's the chisme angle wearing a suit.
      The real test: does the Economic Impact section contain an actual number, mechanism, or
      structural explanation — or just a restated emotional claim?
- [ ] One tight argument, not five loosely connected observations — if the draft is trying to
      make multiple separate points, it's two articles, not one unfocused one

## Tone enforcement (mechanical check against EDITORIAL.md's banned list)

Scan every draft for: "incredible," "amazing," "stunning," "iconic," "the GOAT," "legend,"
"all-time great," "drama," "chaos," "meltdown" used as neutral descriptors, and exclamation
marks in body copy. Any hit gets rewritten — this list exists precisely because these words
are how fan-voice sneaks back in under deadline pressure.

## Expert-sourced content (new, from the personalization/follow-experts feature)

When an article incorporates a take from an "expert" the platform surfaces (pulled from X or
elsewhere per the new personalization system):

- [ ] Verify the account is actually who it claims to be — impersonation and parody accounts
      are common in F1 Twitter/X specifically, confirm before attributing a claim to a real
      named individual
- [ ] Distinguish a credentialed insider (ex-driver, engineer, journalist with sourcing) from
      a popular fan-analyst account — both can be interesting, but the article must be honest
      about which one it's quoting, never implying insider credibility a source doesn't have
- [ ] Never let a pulled quote replace PaddockIntel's own reporting/analysis obligation — an
      aggregated expert take is supporting material for the argument, not a substitute for
      doing the economic/data work described in EDITORIAL.md's Economic Impact section

## Competitive posture ("attacking the big players")

- [ ] The differentiation is substance, not tone — PaddockIntel wins by being more rigorous
      and more sourced than bigger outlets, not by being louder or more provocative. A
      contrarian headline with a thin argument underneath is the exact failure mode this
      advisor exists to catch, regardless of how much traffic it might drive short-term
- [ ] When directly comparing PaddockIntel's take to a competitor's coverage of the same
      event, do it by being right and specific (cite the gap in their analysis concretely),
      never by naming and disparaging the competitor's brand or writers personally

## Structure compliance (cross-check against EDITORIAL.md, don't duplicate here)

Confirm the five-section structure (What Happened → Why It Happened → Economic Impact →
The Framework → Verdict) is intact, stat callouts are present and sourced, FAQ section
answers real "People Also Ask"-shaped questions. If any section is missing or thin, that's a
structural failure this advisor flags even if the prose quality is otherwise fine.

## When this advisor should block publication

Fan-voice language present, angle sentence can't be written, unverified expert attribution,
or opinion presented as fact outside the Verdict section — hold the piece:
