## What happened

The Las Vegas Grand Prix weekend started without a single lap time. Heavy rain that the forecast gave only a 1% chance of, according to F1Mania, soaked the Strip circuit before first practice, leaving standing water, aquaplaning and a track temperature of about 14 degrees Celsius. Seventeen drivers went out on full wet tyres, but none attempted a flying lap, The Race reports. Rafael Câmara and the two Alpines topped the lap count with two each, the Alpines adding practice starts at the end, while Lando Norris, Oscar Piastri, Liam Lawson, Arvid Lindblad and Lance Stroll did not run at all, according to RacingNews365. Sergio Pérez is classified first only because he was the first to complete an out lap.

The lost hour came on top of a grid already reshaped by penalties. Piastri drops 10 places for his fifth internal combustion engine of the season. Carlos Sainz drops 10 for a fifth turbocharger, after Williams fitted Mercedes' new ADUO turbo to both cars, Formula1.com reports; Alex Albon stays within his allocation. Kimi Antonelli takes a new engine as well as the new turbo and drops five places for his sixth combustion engine, Crash.net and RacingNews365 report. George Russell, who chose not to run the new turbo, has no penalty.

Two more followed on Thursday night. Oliver Bearman takes a new combustion engine, turbocharger and MGU-K, a 35-place penalty that becomes a start from the back of the grid, and Lance Stroll drops 10 places for a new energy store and control electronics, RacingNews365 reports. That makes five drivers, almost a quarter of the grid. Second practice ran mostly on intermediates on a slowly drying track: Russell was fastest in 1m47.272s, 0.071s ahead of Pierre Gasly, with Charles Leclerc third, The Race reports, and Max Verstappen 13th.

## Why it happened

The first three penalties are the cost of Mercedes' ADUO upgrade arriving with three races left. Mercedes made the new turbo available to its works team and its customers, McLaren, Williams and Alpine, and every team had to decide whether the small gain justified using up allocation. Russell, third in the drivers' standings and 17 points behind Max Verstappen, is the one who refused. Antonelli, already champion, can afford to start lower.

The washout matters because Las Vegas is the hardest circuit of the year to get tyres working. Teams lose an hour of running on a cold, low-grip track, and the new parts brought for this weekend, including McLaren's 2027-foundation upgrade and a new rear wing at Haas that The Race says it could not test, now have one dry session less before qualifying. Second practice follows later on Thursday night.

Starting at the back is not fatal here. In PaddockIntel's database, Pérez finished third from 11th in 2023, Hamilton second from tenth in 2024 and Antonelli third from 17th in 2025. Across 2026, drivers from Mercedes, Red Bull, Ferrari and McLaren who started 15th or lower scored points every time, seven out of seven, with an average finish of about fifth: Antonelli won at Monza from 19th and Verstappen was second in Brazil from 21st.

## Economic impact

Four of the five penalties land on fights that carry prize money. McLaren is 17 points behind Red Bull for third in the constructors' standings, and Piastri now starts ten places lower than wherever he qualifies. "It's a hell of a lot of money from P4 to P3," Norris told RaceFans, adding that part of it goes to staff as end-of-year bonuses. Williams is ninth, five points ahead of Aston Martin, and Sainz's penalty leaves most of its points-scoring chances with Albon, although Stroll's drop now hurts Aston Martin in the same fight. Haas leads Audi by three points for seventh and starts Bearman from the back.

Antonelli's penalty has no money attached. Antonelli's five-place drop is a Mercedes investment in 2027 reliability, paid for with a race result that changes nothing in the standings, except possibly for Russell, whose fight for second depends on finishing ahead of Verstappen, not his team-mate.

## The framework

Grid penalties in the last races of a season are rarely about the current year. Teams spend allocation they no longer need to protect and test parts for the next car. The useful question is who pays: here it is McLaren and Williams, still fighting for positions, more than Mercedes, which has nothing left to win.

Our simulation from Wednesday, which drew the last three races from this season's results, gave Red Bull third in 81% of runs and Williams ninth in 99%. It did not include penalties. Piastri's makes McLaren's job harder this weekend, but on 2026 evidence a top-team car starting 15th or lower has scored every time, so the bigger risk to McLaren remains Red Bull's form, not the grid drop.

## Verdict

The data says the penalties shift points at the margins, not the fights themselves. A top-team car starting 15th or lower has scored in all seven cases this year, and Las Vegas has produced podiums from 10th, 11th and 17th.

My read: the washout hurts more than the penalties. On the coldest, slipperiest circuit of the year, losing an hour of running punishes the teams with new parts to understand, and McLaren, which brought new parts, is the team that did not even go out.
