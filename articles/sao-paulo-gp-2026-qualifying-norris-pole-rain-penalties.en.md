## What Happened

Lando Norris took pole position for the São Paulo Grand Prix with 1:27.470 on intermediate tyres, 0.204s ahead of George Russell, according to Formula 1's timing. It is his fourth pole of 2026 and, in PaddockIntel's database, the 20th of his career. Kimi Antonelli, the new champion, was third, 0.625s back, Charles Leclerc fourth and Liam Lawson fifth, with Nico Hülkenberg sixth for Audi, Isack Hadjar seventh, Oliver Bearman eighth, Fernando Alonso ninth and Alex Albon tenth.

The grid will look different. Russell takes a five-place penalty for his sixth engine of the season and Leclerc a ten-place penalty for his fifth, so Antonelli joins Norris on the front row, Lawson starts third and Hülkenberg fourth, RaceFans and Motorsport.com report. Lewis Hamilton, eliminated in Q2 in 11th, starts from the back after Ferrari changed most of his power unit during the session, adding 65 places to the ten he already had. Max Verstappen, who did not run in Q2 and was classified 16th, also starts at the back with a 30-place penalty, as does Sergio Pérez. The FIA's provisional starting grid puts Hamilton 20th, Verstappen 21st and Pérez 22nd.

## Why It Happened

Rain decided almost everything. Race control declared the track wet before the session, and Q1 was red-flagged two and a half minutes in as heavy rain left standing water; drivers reported aquaplaning, Motorsport.com notes. The stoppage lasted about 84 minutes. When Q1 resumed with 15 minutes left, the track improved lap by lap, and Norris led the segment with 1:30.939, around ten seconds faster than the first laps after the restart.

The fastest lap of the day came in Q2, not Q3: Russell's 1:26.745 on intermediates, after Alonso became the first driver to switch from full wets. Q2 ended early when Rafael Câmara, making his debut for Haas at his home race, aquaplaned at Junção and went nose-first into the barrier. The session was not resumed. Câmara still finished 13th, ahead of Arvid Lindblad and Carlos Sainz, and after the penalties he starts 11th. Gabriel Bortoleto, the other Brazilian, went off at the same corner in Q1 and was classified last. Rain returned in Q3, and Norris's first run held. Russell was incensed that Mercedes asked him to abort his second flying lap, Motorsport.com reports.

## Economic Impact

The grid penalties land in two money fights. Mercedes leads Ferrari by 126 points in the constructors' championship with 129 left after Brazil, so it clinches the title on Sunday if it outscores Ferrari by three. In PaddockIntel's database, Mercedes has won 12 of the 19 Grands Prix this year to Ferrari's two, and Ferrari now starts the race with Leclerc in the second half of the grid and Hamilton at the back.

Further down, Haas leads Audi by four points for seventh, a gap worth millions in prize money. Hülkenberg starts fourth, the best grid position Audi has had all year (its previous best in 2026 was eighth), while Bortoleto starts near the back. Haas has Bearman ahead of Russell on the grid and a debutant in 11th. And Lawson, whose seat at Racing Bulls is under review against Nikola Tsolov, starts third, equalling the best grid position of his career, from Baku in 2025.

## The Framework

Interlagos has rewarded pole less than most circuits. In PaddockIntel's database, 11 of the 25 Grands Prix there from 2000 to 2025 were won from pole, and three winners started outside the top five: Giancarlo Fisichella from eighth in 2003, Hamilton from tenth in 2021 and Verstappen from 17th in the wet 2024 race. Norris won here from pole last year.

Hülkenberg knows the place in the rain better than most. His only Formula 1 pole, in PaddockIntel's database, came at Interlagos in 2010. Sixteen years later he qualified sixth and starts fourth. Antonelli, who needs nothing more this season, starts second with the team still needing points; Russell, fastest in Q2, starts seventh with the same job.

## Verdict

The data says Norris earned the pole in the only window that mattered, the first Q3 run before the rain came back. My read: the grid is less a ranking than a list of who did not take an engine penalty, and that favours Mercedes on the one day it can close the constructors' title. With Leclerc and Hamilton starting behind more than ten cars and Verstappen at the back, Ferrari has to keep Mercedes from outscoring it by three points from those grid slots. If the weather is the same on Sunday, the 2024 race showed how far a car can come from the back at Interlagos.
