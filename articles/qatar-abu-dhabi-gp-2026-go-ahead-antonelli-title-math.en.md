## What happened

[CONFIRM: date, wording and source of the announcement before publishing.] Formula 1 has confirmed that the 2026 season will end as scheduled, with the Qatar Grand Prix on November 29 and the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix on December 6. The decision lands at the mid-October deadline team principals had been given, after an earlier mid-September target passed without one.

It closes a week in which the opposite looked possible. On October 12, Motorsport.com reported that F1 was moving to replace both races with a season finale at Imola, where temporary grandstands were said to be under construction. The same weekend, Mercedes boss Toto Wolff said his team was preparing "not for four, but six races." The security backdrop had sharpened: RacingNews365 reported four strikes on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport in Saudi Arabia, and the UK advised against all but essential travel to Riyadh, Jeddah and Dammam. [CONFIRM: current UK/US travel advice for Qatar and the UAE.]

## Why it happened

The deciding factor was always the contract. As we set out in September, [F1's hosting agreements assign the cost of a cancellation to whoever cancels](/f1-2026-qatar-abu-dhabi-hosting-fee-standoff/): a promoter that withdraws still owes Formula One Management its fee, while FOM forfeits the fee if it pulls the race itself. Neither Qatar nor Abu Dhabi moved to cancel, and both are state-backed promoters for whom an F1 date is a tourism and visibility asset, not only a ticketing business.

Both contracts run well past this season -- Abu Dhabi to 2030 and Qatar to 2031, according to PlanetF1's contract tracker -- so the decision also protects two long-term relationships. Abu Dhabi has hosted 17 Grands Prix since 2009 and has closed the season since 2014; Qatar has hosted four since its debut in 2021.

## Economic impact

The headline number is what F1 keeps. Neither fee is disclosed, but industry estimates put Qatar's at around $55 million a year and Abu Dhabi's at around $42 million -- close to $100 million combined. Reported estimates put F1's total exposure from losing both rounds, including hospitality, sponsorship and lost earnings around the events, at $190 million to $200 million. Racing keeps that on the books.

It doesn't make the risk free. Formula E's handling of its own December race in Jeddah shows where the cost sits for teams: RacingNews365 reports the bigger obstacle there is insuring UK- and US-based staff to travel under a government advisory, not the race itself. [CONFIRM: whether F1 or teams have disclosed any additional security or insurance arrangements.] The Imola contingency also becomes a sunk cost: F1 said it would pay for preparing Imola as a finale venue even if that work went unused, and no figure for it has been published.

## The framework

For the championship, keeping six races keeps the arithmetic wide. Andrea Kimi Antonelli leaves Singapore on 340 points, 102 clear of George Russell (238), 111 ahead of Lewis Hamilton (229), 119 ahead of Max Verstappen (221) and 125 ahead of Charles Leclerc (215). Six Grands Prix with no Sprints leave 150 points available.

After the United States Grand Prix on October 25, 125 points will remain. Our own calculation from the standings: Antonelli can only clinch in Austin by winning, and then only if Russell finishes ninth or lower and Hamilton fifth or lower. Verstappen and Leclerc can't stop an Austin win on their own. If Russell finishes exactly ninth, he could still tie on points by winning all five remaining races -- and Antonelli would take the title on countback, nine wins to eight. Any other result moves the decision to Mexico City or later.

## Verdict

The data points to a decision driven by contracts more than by the calendar. F1 keeps roughly $100 million in fees it would otherwise have forfeited, and two state promoters keep dates their governments want. My read: the cost of that choice falls less on F1's ledger than on the teams' travel and insurance, and on a title fight that stays mathematically open for at least one more race than it would have with Imola.
