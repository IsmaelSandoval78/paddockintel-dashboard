Isack Hadjar returns to a [Red Bull](/constructors/red_bull/) seat this weekend in Baku that, for three races, wasn't his to sit in. The seat was still his to keep.

Liam Lawson filled in well. That's not the story. The story is that it didn't matter enough to change anything.

## What Happened

Hadjar fractured his wrist during gym training in Formula 1's August break, shortly before the Dutch Grand Prix. He missed three consecutive rounds — the Dutch, Italian and Spanish Grands Prix — while [Liam Lawson](/drivers/lawson/), promoted from Racing Bulls, took his seat at Red Bull. Reserve driver Yuki Tsunoda covered Lawson's vacated seat at Racing Bulls in turn.

Hadjar completed a final simulator assessment ahead of the Azerbaijan Grand Prix without any physical concerns and was cleared to return. The whole substitution chain resolves back to its starting point this weekend, three races and about four weeks after it began.

## Why It Happened

The mechanics were straightforward: an F1 team needs a medically cleared driver in the car, and Red Bull had one sitting inside its own junior program rather than needing to reach outside it. Lawson had raced at the senior team before (as Verstappen's teammate for part of 2025), which made him the lowest-friction substitute — familiar with the car, the garage, and the sponsor obligations that come with a Red Bull race seat.

What made this substitution worth watching, rather than a routine reshuffle, is what it put at stake alongside it: Red Bull's actual 2027 driver decisions were reportedly already in progress on a similar timeline, according to team-adjacent reporting. A three-race audition landing in the middle of that window gave Lawson a real, if compressed, chance to make his case with results instead of arguments.

## Economic Impact

Lawson took that chance. Across the three races he scored **14 points** — P7 at Zandvoort, a rough Monza weekend that started from **P22** on the grid and ended P14, and P6 at the Spanish Grand Prix. That is a genuinely competitive stand-in record, not a placeholder performance.

It didn't reopen the decision. Team principal Laurent Mekies praised how quickly Lawson adapted, in an on-the-record interview, but continued to frame Hadjar as Red Bull's long-term pick for the senior seat alongside Max Verstappen. The reason traces back to the number that mattered before the injury ever happened: Hadjar sits **8th in the championship with 68 points** from his rookie season — a full year's evidence, banked before he ever got hurt.

That is the actual mechanism here, and it's a market-structure one, not a sentimental one. F1 teams price a driver's value off a full season's sample, not a short substitute window, however good that window looks. Red Bull had already spent a year building a public case for Hadjar as Verstappen's long-term teammate; reopening that after three races — even three good ones — would mean discarding sunk investment in a decision the team had already made public commitments around. Reporting on the 2027 lineup points to Lawson's most likely landing spot as Racing Bulls again, paired with junior driver Arvid Lindblad, with Nikola Tsolov in a reserve role — a shape that was reportedly on the table before Hadjar's injury created this detour.

## The Framework

F1's driver market runs on accumulated evidence, not audition windows. A rookie season's worth of results is close to impossible for a three-race substitute stint to outweigh, no matter how clean the stand-in's numbers are — the sample sizes aren't remotely comparable, and teams know it.

That asymmetry is structural, not personal to Lawson. Any driver stepping into an incumbent's seat for a short, injury-forced window is working against a full season of banked results on the other side of the comparison, plus whatever public and sponsor commitments the team has already made around that incumbent. The substitute's job, in market terms, isn't to outscore the incumbent's season — it's not to embarrass the team while the incumbent recovers. Lawson did that job well. It was never going to be enough to change the outcome.

## Verdict

Fourteen points, two point-scoring finishes out of three, and a decision that didn't move. Lawson's stand-in run was real evidence of talent — it just wasn't evidence relevant to the question Red Bull had already answered.

My read: watch what Red Bull actually does with Lawson for 2027, not what this window seemed to open up. A strong caretaker stint is a good audition for a different seat than the one it was filling — and reporting on the 2027 lineup already points to exactly that: Racing Bulls, not a Red Bull promotion.
