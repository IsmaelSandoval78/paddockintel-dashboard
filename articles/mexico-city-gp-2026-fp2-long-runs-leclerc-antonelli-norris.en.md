## What Happened

Charles Leclerc was fastest in both Friday practice sessions at the Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez. In FP1 his 1:18.128 was 0.659s clear of Kimi Antonelli. In FP2 he lapped in 1:17.827 on soft tyres, 0.103s ahead of Lando Norris and 0.106s ahead of George Russell, with Antonelli fourth, 0.217s back, and Max Verstappen fifth, 0.311s back, according to the official timing. Antonelli lost almost all of his deficit in the first sector, 0.180s of the 0.217s, and on that lap he was the slowest of the top five through the speed trap, at 337 km/h against Leclerc's 346.

The single lap is not the most useful thing FP2 produces. It is the session where teams run their race simulations, and PaddockIntel analysed every lap with FastF1, which reads Formula 1's official timing feed. **On the medium tyre, Antonelli averaged 1:21.487 over 10 laps and Leclerc 1:21.501 over 12: a gap of 0.014s.** On softs, Norris set the best average of the session, 1:21.319 over 11 laps, ahead of Russell (1:21.442 over nine) and Verstappen (1:21.490 over seven).

The session was stopped once. Verstappen and Liam Lawson came through Turn 12 side by side, neither backed out, and the contact shed debris that brought a short red flag, Formula1.com reported. Later Lewis Hamilton was told to stop his Ferrari on track under a Virtual Safety Car. "Something's wrong with the power. I lost all power from Turn 13," he said on the radio. He finished 11th after 13 laps. The track stayed dry, and 14 of 542 timed laps were deleted for track limits.

## Why It Happened

A long run can only be compared with another on the same compound, and the medium-tyre comparison is as close as practice data gets. Antonelli and Leclerc ran the same tyre over similar distances, and their lap times rose at almost the same rate, 0.123s and 0.125s a lap. Leclerc's 12 laps include one deleted for track limits; as in PaddockIntel's earlier race-week analyses, deleted laps stay in the average, because a deletion is a sporting sanction, not a change in the pace being measured. The method counts green-flag laps only, drops out-laps, in-laps and any lap more than 3% off the stint median, and needs at least five laps for a stint to count.

On softs, the order depends on how long each run lasted. Norris lost 0.076s a lap over 11 laps and Russell 0.058s over nine. Verstappen's seven laps, on tyres that were already five laps old, did not slow down at all. Isack Hadjar averaged 1:21.369, second only to Norris, but over five laps, the shortest run in the comparison, and he lost 0.254s a lap. Mercedes split its programme between compounds, Russell on softs and Antonelli on mediums, which means its two drivers cannot be read against each other directly.

The usual warning applies. Fuel loads are not published, so a car that looks quick may simply be lighter. The honest reading is a tie between Antonelli and Leclerc on mediums and a small Norris lead on softs, not a ranking.

## Economic Impact

Ferrari has the most riding on a Friday like this one. SportsPro this week ranked Leclerc ninth in its 50 Most Marketable Athletes for 2026, the highest-placed motorsport athlete, and Interbrand valued the Ferrari brand at $17.7 billion, up 14.7%. The team's results lag behind that: two wins in 18 rounds and second in the constructors' standings with 474 points, 133 behind Mercedes. In PaddockIntel's database, Mercedes takes the constructors' title in Mexico if it outscores Ferrari by 39 points this weekend. Leclerc's pace is Ferrari's best way to push that back, and Hamilton's power failure is the risk on the other side of the garage.

The fight below matters too. Red Bull and McLaren are level on 355 points for third in the constructors' championship, and in the prize-money split each place is worth more than the one behind it. On Friday, McLaren had the best soft-tyre run and Red Bull the flattest one. Verstappen's grid drop costs Red Bull one place on Sunday's grid, and Lawson takes the same penalty at Racing Bulls. Valtteri Bottas drops 50 places, in effect to the back of the grid, after Cadillac fitted his fifth engine and turbocharger and fourth energy store, control electronics and MGU-K of the season, more than the regulations allow, according to the stewards.

## The Framework

Mexico City rewards the front of the grid more than most circuits. In PaddockIntel's database, five of the 10 Grands Prix held on the current layout between 2015 and 2025 were won from pole, and no winner started lower than third. Verstappen won from second in 2017 and 2018 and from third in 2021 and 2023. Leclerc has taken pole there twice, in 2019 and 2023, and lost both races to a driver who started third.

That makes Saturday a big step, but not a decisive one. Leclerc's one-lap margin across both sessions makes him the favourite for pole, and his medium-tyre pace says the car does not fade over a stint. Verstappen's penalty bites only at the margin: a Verstappen who qualifies third starts fourth, a grid slot no Mexico winner on this layout has started from.

The title has its own arithmetic. Antonelli has 365 points to Russell's 242 and Hamilton's 241, with 125 left. He needs two points, a ninth place, whatever they do. If neither Russell nor Hamilton wins on Sunday, he is champion even without scoring; if Hamilton wins, tenth is enough. Russell's soft-tyre run was the second best of the session, so a Russell win is possible on Friday's numbers. Antonelli finishing outside the top nine is the less likely half of the condition: he has finished ninth or better in 16 of 18 races this year.

## Verdict

The data says Mexico is a two-tyre story. Leclerc owns the single lap, Antonelli matches him on the medium, and Norris had the best soft-tyre run of FP2. My read: Leclerc is the favourite for pole, but the medium-tyre tie is the number that matters for the title. It says Antonelli has the race pace to finish well inside the top nine even if his Saturday goes wrong, which leaves the championship needing more than a Russell or Hamilton win to wait another week. The question Friday leaves open is not whether Antonelli becomes champion on Sunday, but whether Ferrari can stop Mercedes from celebrating twice.
