## What Happened

Honda is replacing Tetsushi Kakuda, the engineer who led the power unit program that took Max Verstappen to four consecutive drivers' titles at Red Bull, with his deputy Yoichiro Fukao — effective October 1. Fukao's new title is Large Project Leader. Honda Racing Corporation calls it a "planned transition," but the timing says otherwise: it landed mid-season, not at a program checkpoint, and broke during the same Spanish Grand Prix weekend where FIA president Mohammed Ben Sulayem, Aston Martin owner Lawrence Stroll, and Fernando Alonso reportedly discussed further support for Honda's program.

Fukao has been at Honda since 2002, with a stint on its MotoGP program from 2009 to 2011 before returning to four-wheel work. He has served as deputy F1 project leader twice already, in 2017 and again in 2022. This is not an outsider parachuting in — it is Honda moving its own bench, fast.

The context: Aston Martin, badged Aston Martin Aramco Honda since the partnership began, has 3 points through 14 rounds of 2026 — 10th in the constructors' championship. Alonso is 19th in the drivers' standings. Stroll has yet to score. Neither car finished either of the first three races of the season.

## Why It Happened

The engine at the center of this, internally designated the RA626H, has had severe vibration issues since the first race weekend of 2026 — the direct cause of the early-season retirements. Honda has already tried to fix it once: a "Spec 2" upgrade introduced at Zandvoort gained 0.05 to 0.1 seconds per lap, according to Honda trackside general manager Shintaro Orihara. "We have observed a step forward in terms of performance," Orihara said. "We have confirmed on dyno, so that was a positive thing we found." That is a real gain and a small one — nowhere near enough to move Aston Martin out of the bottom third of the grid.

Honda also quietly sent Aston Martin additional development assistance beyond its standard support protocols back in May, before this leadership change. Two interventions in one season is not a team executing a plan on schedule. It is a manufacturer that started 2026 behind and has been trying to buy back time ever since.

The deeper cause predates this season. Honda announced it was leaving F1 at the end of 2021, then reversed that decision to build the very power unit now underperforming — a stop-start sequence that cost real preparation time against a clean-sheet 2026 regulation reset every rival used every available month to prepare for. Honda also lost engineers when it split from Red Bull Powertrains, a staffing hit compounding into this program's development schedule. Kakuda inherited a compressed timeline; Fukao inherits the same one, six months further behind.

## Economic Impact

Start with what a leadership change cannot buy back: this season's points. Honda has confirmed no further major power unit update is planned before 2027 — the next real technical shot arrives with the next regulatory cycle, not before it. Whatever Fukao does between October and December, Aston Martin's 2026 prize-money position is locked in at roughly where it sits today, near the bottom of a championship where finishing position sets each team's share of F1's centralized revenue pool for the following year. Three points instead of thirty is not a rounding error on that payment.

That makes this move about something other than 2026 results — it is about the Aston Martin Aramco Honda brand itself. Stroll rebuilt the team's identity, sponsorship deck, and visual livery around Honda's name arriving as works partner, betting that "Honda" would read as a competitiveness signal to sponsors and to Alonso, whose own [2027 contract leverage runs directly through whether this partnership looks like it is going somewhere](/fernando-alonso-f1-future-2027-salary-negotiation). A power unit that can't finish races for three straight rounds is the opposite of that signal — the same underperformance PaddockIntel already clocked on [Aston Martin's mirror-image Friday at Madrid's own debut weekend](/madrid-gp-2026-fp1-mercedes-aston-martin) — and it is happening in year one of the exact partnership built to prevent it.

Swapping the technical lead is the fastest, cheapest lever Honda has to demonstrate urgency without the cost or optics of a mid-season driver change or a public admission that the RA626H program is structurally behind. It is a confidence purchase aimed at two audiences that matter more to Honda's F1 economics than this season's constructors' points: Stroll, whose patience funds the partnership, and the FIA and rival manufacturers watching whether Honda's return is a real long-term commitment or another false start like 2021.

## The Framework

This is the standard playbook when a factory engine program underperforms in year one of a new formula: the lever manufacturers pull first is engineering leadership, not the driver lineup or the commercial partnership itself. Drivers are under contract and changing one mid-crisis reads as panic to sponsors. Walking away from the partnership entirely, this early, forfeits years of committed investment for nothing. A technical leadership change is the one move available that is fast, internally controlled, and framable as "acceleration" rather than failure — which is exactly the language Honda used.

The 2021 exit-then-reversal is the part of this story most coverage undersells. A manufacturer's F1 commitment is not a decision it can restart cleanly after pausing it — the engineers, the supplier relationships, and the development calendar do not wait, and the 2026 rules reset punished every team that lost even a few months of preparation. Honda is not paying for this season's vibration problem alone. It is paying, with a year's delay, for a boardroom decision made half a decade ago.

## Verdict

Kakuda's record — four Verstappen titles' worth of power unit development — means this was never a competence problem being quietly admitted. It is a confidence problem being addressed in the only currency Honda can spend before 2027: personnel. My read is that Fukao's real job isn't fixing the RA626H before the season ends, because Honda has already said that isn't happening. His job is convincing Stroll, Alonso, and the paddock that Honda's second F1 era won't repeat its first one — that the exit-and-return pattern is over, and next year's power unit is what a fully-committed manufacturer, not a hedging one, actually builds.
