## What Happened

DAZN extended its Formula 1 broadcast rights across Spain, Portugal, and Andorra through the end of the 2030 season, covering every session across F1, F2, F3, F1 Academy, and Porsche Supercup. The announcement landed on September 8, 2026 — during Madrid's debut Grand Prix weekend, F1's first race in the Spanish capital in 45 years — and ahead of Portugal's return to the calendar at Portimão. Neither side disclosed a fee.

## Why It Happened

The timing isn't a coincidence of scheduling. It's the point. DAZN's previous Spanish rights deal, held by Telefonica through 2023, was reported at roughly €65 million a year — a price set before Spain had two live F1 storylines running at once. Locking in the next six years now, mid-Madrid-weekend, means DAZN buys Iberian F1 rights before this year's ticket demand, TV ratings, and subscriber growth show up in anyone's renewal math.

Portugal adds a second reason to move early. DAZN also takes over Portuguese F1 rights from Sport TV, whose five-year deal runs out after this season — right as Portimão prepares to rejoin the calendar in 2027. A broadcaster negotiating after Portimão's return, with a proven live gate and local storyline already established, is negotiating from a weaker position than one that locks in the rights before that demand exists on paper.

## Economic Impact

The clearest comparison for what DAZN is trying to avoid paying later is what Apple is already paying in the US. Apple TV replaced ESPN as F1's exclusive American broadcaster at $140 million a year — a 65% premium over ESPN's previous rate, priced only after F1's US audience had visibly grown through the Netflix-driven boom of the early 2020s. DAZN's Iberian bet is the mirror image: pay an unknown, presumably smaller number now, before Madrid and Portimão do to Spain and Portugal what several years of US growth did to F1's American rights value.

That bet has a real, disclosed floor to measure against. Telefonica's €65 million-a-year rate is the last confirmed price for Spanish F1 rights, set before the market had a home Grand Prix to sell around. Every additional storyline DAZN's new six-year deal captures — Madrid's opening seasons, Portugal's return, whatever ratings both produce — is upside DAZN locked in at a rate closer to that €65 million baseline than to a post-demand renegotiation.

The downside sits with F1, not DAZN. An undisclosed extension signed before demand is proven means F1 is trading certainty for a lower price today, the same trade every rights holder makes when it prefers a done deal over the leverage of an auction after a market heats up. Whether that trade pays off for F1 depends entirely on whether the number DAZN agreed to actually anticipated a Madrid GP and a returning Portuguese round, or was set before F1 had a strong read on their commercial worth itself.

## The Framework

Live sports rights price on realized demand, not projected demand — which means the party negotiating first, before a new market event proves itself, structurally gets the better rate. Apple's $140 million only happened after ESPN's exclusivity had already run its course and the audience shift to streaming was undeniable. DAZN's Iberian move inverts that sequence deliberately: sign before Madrid's first checkered flag, not after its first season of ratings.

It's the same asymmetry that shapes how F1 prices a new host city's hosting fee before a single race proves out attendance, [as PaddockIntel found in Madrid's own €48 million-a-year hosting deal](/madrid-gp-2026-economic-impact) — the promoter and the broadcaster are both betting on a number set ahead of the evidence, for opposite reasons: F1 wants the certainty of a locked-in host, DAZN wants the certainty of a locked-in price before that same host makes the rights more expensive.

## Verdict

DAZN isn't betting on F1 loyalty in Spain and Portugal — it's betting that six years of Madrid and a returning Portimão will be worth more than whatever it just agreed to pay for them. My read: if Madrid's opening seasons draw anywhere near the numbers PwC projected for the local economy, DAZN's extension will look, in hindsight, like the cheapest rights deal F1 signs in this decade.
