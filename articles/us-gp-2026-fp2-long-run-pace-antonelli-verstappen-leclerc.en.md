## What Happened

Charles Leclerc topped second practice for the United States Grand Prix with a 1:35.291 on soft tyres, 0.042s ahead of Kimi Antonelli and 0.189s ahead of Max Verstappen, according to the official timing. The three best laps were set on different parts of the lap: Verstappen had the quickest first sector of the three, Antonelli the quickest second sector, 0.374s faster than Leclerc, and Leclerc the quickest third sector. Lewis Hamilton was fourth, 0.615s back, and George Russell fifth on mediums.

The one-lap order is not the useful part of FP2. The session is where teams run their race simulations, and PaddockIntel analysed every lap of it with FastF1, which reads the official F1 timing feed. **Verstappen averaged 1:41.481 over 11 laps on soft tyres and Leclerc 1:41.508 over 13: a gap of 0.027s.** Antonelli ran the medium tyre, a harder compound, and averaged 1:41.704 over 10 laps, 0.22s off Verstappen's soft run, with the lowest tyre wear of the leading runs.

Conditions were dry, with the track temperature peaking at 43.2°C. Of 589 laps timed, 91 were deleted for track limits, most of them at Turns 12 and 20.

## Why It Happened

A long run is only comparable within a compound, and the useful number on Friday is how fast a lap time rises as the tyre ages. Leclerc's soft run lost 0.090s a lap and Verstappen's 0.074s. Antonelli's medium run lost 0.047s a lap, and his teammate Russell, on the same compound, lost 0.461s a lap over a shorter five-lap run. Lando Norris posted the quickest average of the session, 1:41.456 on hard tyres, but six of his eight laps were deleted for track limits and his run degraded 0.254s a lap.

Two caveats decide how far these numbers go. Fuel loads are not published, so a car that looks quick may simply be lighter. And PaddockIntel kept laps deleted for track limits in the averages, because a deletion is a sporting sanction, not a change in the pace being measured. Removing them reverses Verstappen and Leclerc and leaves Antonelli's and Norris's runs with fewer than five laps. That is why the honest reading is a dead heat between Verstappen and Leclerc, not a ranking.

## Economic Impact

Friday is the first look at the last development money of the year. Every team works inside the $215 million cost cap, and the Austin weekend is where several of them chose to spend their final upgrades. Ferrari's FIA submission lists a new board brace, a redesigned front floor leading edge, reshaped floorboards, a new sidepod inlet and undercut and a revised diffuser, The Race reports, even though Hamilton called it "half a tenth". Red Bull brought a single front-wing vane for evaluation. Mercedes runs its Sepang chassis package on both cars for the first time, and has pushed its ADUO engine upgrade back to Brazil at the earliest.

On Friday's evidence, Ferrari's spend bought a car level with Red Bull's on a soft-tyre run. Red Bull reached the same pace with almost nothing new. For Ferrari, 105 points ahead of McLaren for second in the constructors' championship, that is a step that protects a position. For Red Bull, it confirms that its own power unit, in its first season, is now the reference other manufacturers are measured against.

The title has its own arithmetic. Antonelli leads Russell by 102 points and clinches on Sunday only if he wins, Russell finishes ninth or lower and Hamilton fifth or lower. Friday's long runs make the first condition possible and the other two unlikely: Hamilton's soft run averaged 1:42.130, about 0.6s off Leclerc, which is still pace for the top five, and Russell's medium pace was competitive before his tyres faded.

## The Framework

COTA rewards race pace more than most tracks. In PaddockIntel's database, only 6 of the 13 United States Grands Prix held there from 2012 to 2025 were won from pole. Verstappen won from sixth on the grid in 2023 and Leclerc from fourth in 2024. A team that is level on Saturday but kinder to its tyres has a real route to the win on Sunday.

That is why the medium-tyre run matters more than its slightly slower average suggests. A harder compound that loses only 0.047s a lap gives a team options on strategy: a longer first stint, a later stop, a fresher tyre at the end. Practice data cannot show fuel loads or engine modes, but tyre wear is the one number that carries over from Friday to Sunday almost unchanged, because the asphalt and the compounds do not change.

## Verdict

The data says Austin is a three-car race. Verstappen and Leclerc are inseparable on the soft tyre, and Antonelli is within a quarter of a second on a harder one with the least wear of the three. My read: the medium run is the one that matters for the title, because it gives Mercedes the strategic freedom a win needs. Whether the title is settled on Sunday depends less on Antonelli than on where Russell and Hamilton finish, and Friday's numbers give no reason to expect both of them to fall that far.
