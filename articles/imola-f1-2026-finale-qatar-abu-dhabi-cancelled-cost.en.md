## What happened

[CONFIRM: date, wording and source of the announcement before publishing.] Formula 1 has cancelled the 2026 Qatar Grand Prix (November 29) and Abu Dhabi Grand Prix (December 6) and will end the season at Imola on December 6, the date Abu Dhabi held. The 2026 calendar, which began with 24 rounds and already lost Bahrain's and Saudi Arabia's April dates, now finishes with 22.

Motorsport.com first reported the plan on October 12, saying Imola had been on standby for months and that temporary grandstands were already being built. The security backdrop had sharpened over the same weekend: RacingNews365 reported four strikes on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport in Saudi Arabia, and the UK advised against all but essential travel to Riyadh, Jeddah and Dammam. [CONFIRM: the specific reason F1 gives in its statement.]

## Why it happened

As we set out in September, the decision was always going to be paced by [a contract standoff over who cancels first](/f1-2026-qatar-abu-dhabi-hosting-fee-standoff/). Under F1's hosting agreements, a promoter that withdraws still owes Formula One Management its fee; if FOM cancels instead, it forfeits that fee. Both promoters are state-backed and both have long contracts -- Abu Dhabi to 2030, Qatar to 2031, per PlanetF1 -- which is why neither had moved to cancel on its own. [CONFIRM: who formally cancelled, and whether force majeure was invoked.]

Imola was the ready answer. The circuit hosted the Emilia Romagna Grand Prix in five seasons between 2020 and 2025 and lost its place on the 2026 calendar when its contract ran out. In PaddockIntel's database it has hosted 32 World Championship races since 1980, under the San Marino, Emilia Romagna and Italian Grand Prix names. It has never hosted a season finale.

## Economic impact

What F1 gives up is the bigger number. Neither fee is disclosed, but industry estimates put Qatar's at around $55 million a year and Abu Dhabi's at around $42 million -- close to $100 million combined. Reported estimates put F1's total exposure from losing both rounds at $190 million to $200 million once hospitality, sponsorship and lost earnings are included. How much of that F1 actually absorbs depends on who formally cancelled, which the contracts decide. [CONFIRM: any disclosed terms.]

Against that, Imola is a one-off with its own cost line. F1 said before the decision that it would pay to prepare Imola as a finale venue, and no figure for that work or for any Imola hosting fee has been published. The one precedent from this season points to the upside on the gate: the Bahrain Grand Prix relocated to Sepang drew 261,000 over its weekend, against Bahrain's own record of 105,000 -- though Bahrain paid for that move itself, and no Gulf promoter has been reported as funding Imola.

## The framework

The calendar change also rewrites the title arithmetic. Andrea Kimi Antonelli leaves Singapore on 340 points, 102 clear of George Russell (238), 111 ahead of Lewis Hamilton (229), 119 ahead of Max Verstappen (221), 125 ahead of Charles Leclerc (215) and 139 ahead of Lando Norris (201). With five races left instead of six, 125 points remain, not 150.

That eliminates two drivers immediately. Norris can't reach 340. Leclerc could only tie it by winning all five remaining races while Antonelli scored nothing, and would then lose on countback, eight wins to six. After Austin, only 100 points will remain. Our calculation from the standings: Antonelli is champion in Austin unless Russell outscores him there by three points or more, Hamilton by 12 or more, or Verstappen by 20 or more. A win clinches it outright. Even a retirement would be enough if Russell finishes ninth or lower, Hamilton fifth or lower and Verstappen doesn't win.

## Verdict

The data points to an expensive decision on F1's side of the ledger and a cheap one for the championship leader. F1 walks away from close to $100 million a year in reported fees for two of its longest contracts, and takes on an unbudgeted finale at a circuit it had just dropped. My read: the money will be settled in the contracts, out of public view. The title, on this calendar, is now Antonelli's to lose in Austin.
