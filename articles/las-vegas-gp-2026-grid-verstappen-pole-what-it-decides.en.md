## What happened

Kimi Antonelli was fastest in qualifying for the Las Vegas Grand Prix, but Max Verstappen starts the race on pole. Antonelli's 1m33.893s beat Verstappen by 0.258s, and his five-place penalty for new power unit components drops him to sixth on the grid published by RaceFans. George Russell starts alongside Verstappen, with Lando Norris, Isack Hadjar and Arvid Lindblad behind.

The penalties reshape the rest of the order. Oscar Piastri, seventh fastest, starts 17th; Carlos Sainz starts 20th, Lance Stroll 21st and Oliver Bearman last. Both Ferraris went out in Q2 and start 10th and 11th, Charles Leclerc ahead of Lewis Hamilton. In PaddockIntel's database it is the first time this season that neither Ferrari reached Q3.

The stewards fined Racing Bulls €5,000 for releasing Lindblad unsafely in Q2, with no grid penalty, Motorsport.com reports, so the order stands. The race runs on Saturday night, local time.

## Why it happened

Mercedes chose speed over grid position. It fitted its new ADUO turbo to Antonelli, who is already champion and can afford five places, and not to Russell, who is fighting for second in the drivers' championship, Crash.net reported earlier this week. The plan was then for Antonelli to give Russell a tow on the final Q3 runs, but it fell apart when both cars got caught in a pack, Motorsport.com reports: Antonelli backed off at Turn 5, a decision he said was "fully on me", and used other cars' slipstream to go fastest. Toto Wolff said Russell still "had the perfect position". Russell missed pole by 0.073s.

Ferrari's problem was tyre temperature: both drivers struggled to warm the tyres in FP3 and qualifying, Motorsport.com reports. Leclerc then made an error at Turn 7 on his last Q2 lap, The Race reports, and Hamilton's side of the garage reported a lack of energy at the end of his lap, according to Formula1.it. Verstappen, who complained of the "same problem as Brazil" on Thursday, took pole after receiving a driver warning from the stewards for an incident with Russell in final practice.

## Economic impact

Formula 1 pays its prize fund by constructors' position, so the grid matters most where teams are close; the exact amounts per position are not published. Red Bull, 17 points ahead of McLaren for third, starts first and fourth, while McLaren starts third and 17th. "It's a hell of a lot of money from P4 to P3," Norris told RaceFans this week.

Further down, the grid tilts the fight for seventh against Haas. Haas leads Audi by three points but starts 15th and 22nd, with Audi 12th and 13th. In the 20 Grands Prix of 2026, Haas scored in seven and Audi in nine. Williams, five points ahead of Aston Martin for ninth, starts 14th and 20th against 18th and 21st. Those places carry money for teams that Forbes values at $2.8 billion, in Haas's case, and $3.8 billion, in Audi's.

## The framework

We reran Wednesday's simulation with the real grid. For Las Vegas, every driver starts from his grid slot and gains or loses a number of places drawn at random from his own 2026 Grands Prix, finish minus grid, with retirements kept as retirements; the results are ranked and scored 25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1. Rafael Câmara, with one race, uses Haas's combined record. Qatar and Abu Dhabi are then drawn from this season's 20 races, as on Wednesday, and the whole process is repeated 100,000 times.

The results: Verstappen keeps second in about 82% of runs, Russell takes it in 14%, Hamilton in 2% and Norris in 1%, with about 2% ending level. Russell leaves Las Vegas ahead of Verstappen in only about 8% of runs. Red Bull keeps third in 93%, up from 81% before qualifying. Haas holds seventh in 49%, Audi takes it in 45% and 6% end level, which Haas would currently win on countback. Williams keeps ninth in 96%.

The points math is simple at the front. Russell needs to beat Verstappen by 18 points to lead, or by 17 for a tie that he would win on wins, four to three: a Russell victory with Verstappen sixth or lower does it. Ferrari can clinch second in the constructors' table only if Red Bull does not outscore it by nine points or more, and from 10th and 11th that happens in only about 13% of runs; Ferrari still keeps second in practically every simulation.

Las Vegas history argues against reading too much into pole. The pole-sitter has won one of three editions, while every winner started on the front row. Recoveries are common: Antonelli finished third from 17th in 2025, Lance Stroll fifth from 19th in 2023 and Hamilton second from tenth in 2024. Across 2026, drivers starting 10th or 11th scored in 21 of 40 starts, and Ferrari cars starting tenth or lower finished ninth, eighth and 15th. The model ignores track-specific pace and does not know how strong each car is beyond each driver's own record.

## Verdict

The data says the grid made Verstappen and Red Bull clear favourites in their fights. Second in the drivers' standings stays with Verstappen in more than four runs out of five, and third in the constructors' table is now Red Bull's to lose. The open fight is seventh, where Bearman's penalty turns a three-point Haas lead into a coin toss.

My read: the first corner decides more than the simulation can. Russell starts next to the rival he touched wheels with on Friday, both know a warning is already on Verstappen's record, and Russell needs a big swing in one race. If he does not lead after Turn 1, second is very likely settled for Verstappen.
