## What Happened

Lando Norris won the Brazilian Grand Prix from pole for McLaren in 1:35:23.504, 14.6 seconds ahead of Max Verstappen, who started 21st after an engine penalty, according to the FIA's final classification. Isack Hadjar was third, giving Red Bull two drivers on the podium. George Russell finished fourth and Kimi Antonelli fifth, and that was enough for Mercedes to clinch the constructors' championship with three races left, its ninth title, level with Williams, RaceFans reports.

Oscar Piastri was sixth, almost a minute behind his teammate. Liam Lawson was classified seventh and Charles Leclerc eighth: Leclerc passed Lawson on the penultimate lap, but the stewards gave him a five-second penalty for the contact at Turn 12 that let him close up, ruling it had "an obvious sporting consequence". Antonelli and Oliver Bearman also received five-second penalties for unsafe releases, which did not change their positions. Lewis Hamilton finished ninth, a lap down, on his 400th Formula 1 start, and Nico Hülkenberg took the last point despite a ten-second penalty for gaining an advantage off the track. Rafael Câmara finished 20th on his debut for Haas, Lance Stroll retired for the 14th time this season, and Valtteri Bottas did not start after a wheel nut on his Cadillac jammed on the grid. It is Norris's third win of 2026 and, in PaddockIntel's database, the 14th of his career and his 50th podium. "The main thing today was I just kept it cool and I didn't make any mistakes," he said.

## Why It Happened

The race started on intermediates on a wet track. Antonelli beat Norris into Turn 1 and Lawson followed him through; Norris took second back on lap 7, RacingNews365 reports. In Formula 1's timing, Antonelli led the first 19 laps and pulled clear. Hamilton, who had gained 11 places on the opening lap to run ninth, was the first driver to switch to slicks at the end of lap 15, and the whole field followed within four laps. Russell spun at the pit exit and lost four places: "I probably just took too much risk, because I was seeing an opportunity to go for the win," he told Formula1.com.

The race turned at the start of lap 20, when Antonelli spun out of the lead at Turn 1. He had felt "something weird with the car" since fitting softs, he told The Race: "I don't know why I lost the rear so quickly. Like, no warning, nothing." Hadjar and Verstappen passed him over the next laps. Verstappen, 13th after lap 1, sixth by lap 10 and fourth by lap 20, was let through by Hadjar on lap 38 on Red Bull's instruction, stopped again on lap 46 and held second, though he said the car felt "like the complete opposite" after his first stop and he "couldn't really make any inroads" on Norris. "No sim in the world will predict that you will go from P21 to P2," said Red Bull team principal Laurent Mekies. Rain returned from lap 62, the leaders switched to intermediates a lap later, and Norris controlled the final laps. Hamilton stayed out a lap too long and spun on slicks before pitting; he said he lacked information about the rain and added, "ultimately, that's on me."

## Economic Impact

The constructors' title is settled at 652 points to Ferrari's 510 in the FIA's standings, a lead of 142 with 129 left. In Brazil Mercedes needed to outscore Ferrari by three points and did so 22 to 6. "Those last four years were very difficult," Toto Wolff told Sky Sports, adding that few teams in any sport had come back to win after a long title run and a difficult spell. Every other position in the constructors' table, and the prize money attached to it, is still open: Ferrari leads Red Bull by 95 points for second, Red Bull leads McLaren by 17 for third after the double podium, and Hülkenberg's point cut Haas's lead over Audi for seventh to three. Leclerc's penalty moved two points from Ferrari to Racing Bulls.

The drivers' runner-up spot is closer still. Verstappen moved to 279 points, ahead of Russell on 262, Hamilton on 261, Leclerc on 249 and Norris on 246: five drivers within 33 points, with 75 left to each of them. Lawson, whose Racing Bulls seat is still undecided, ran second early and took seventh.

## The Framework

Verstappen has now reached the Interlagos podium from the back of the field three years running: a win from 17th in 2024, third from the pit lane in 2025 and second from 21st today. In PaddockIntel's database, 21st is the deepest grid position from which any driver has reached the podium there; before today it was Carlos Sainz's 20th in 2019. Six podiums at Interlagos have come from 14th on the grid or lower, and three of them are Verstappen's. The front of the grid still won: 20 of the 43 Grands Prix at Interlagos went to the driver who started first, and Norris won here from pole last year too.

Two milestones sat inside the result. Hamilton's 400 starts are second only to Fernando Alonso, for whom Interlagos was start number 445, Formula1.com notes. Hadjar's third place is his third podium of his first season at Red Bull.

## Verdict

The data says the race was decided by handling, not pace: Antonelli led comfortably until the car let go without warning, and Norris was close enough to take over when it did. My read: Mercedes won the title on an afternoon when both its cars spun and neither finished on the podium, its first podium-less Grand Prix of 2026, The Race notes, which shows how much of the job was done before Brazil. Ferrari's day was the opposite: a car Leclerc called "just like driving on ice" on the opening stint, and a penalty that cost it two more points. The story people will remember is Verstappen's 21st to second.
