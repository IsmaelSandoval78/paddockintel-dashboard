## What happened

Formula 1 arrives in Las Vegas with both 2026 titles decided and five fights still open. Kimi Antonelli is champion on 390 points and Mercedes clinched the constructors' title in Brazil with 652. Behind them, the FIA's standings after the São Paulo Grand Prix leave a fight for second among the drivers and four in the constructors' table: Ferrari leads Red Bull by 95 points for second, Red Bull leads McLaren by 17 for third, Racing Bulls leads Alpine by 45 for fifth and Haas leads Audi by 3 for seventh. Williams is 5 ahead of Aston Martin for ninth.

The race runs on the night of Saturday, November 21, local time, the first of three Grands Prix in 15 days, with Qatar on November 29 and Abu Dhabi on December 6, none of them with a sprint. Each team can score at most 43 points a weekend, 129 in total, and each driver 25 a race.

The weekend has already moved the grid. Oscar Piastri takes a 10-place penalty for a fifth combustion engine, Autosport reports. Mercedes fits its new ADUO turbo only to Antonelli's car, because George Russell, third in the standings, would take a grid penalty, Crash.net reports; McLaren and the other Mercedes customers run it on both cars. Three teams hand a car to another driver in first practice: Dino Beganovic takes Lewis Hamilton's Ferrari, Ryo Hirakawa Oliver Bearman's Haas and Jak Crawford, the American, Fernando Alonso's Aston Martin in his home race. Rafael Câmara keeps the second Haas for the whole weekend.

## Why it happened

The fights are close because the second half of the season reshuffled the order behind Mercedes. Red Bull had no podium in the first four rounds and won three of the last five, all with Max Verstappen, who has finished on the podium in eight straight Grands Prix in our database. McLaren won three races, all with Lando Norris, but went five rounds without a podium before Brazil.

Las Vegas has rewarded a strong Saturday less than most circuits. In three editions the pole-sitter has won once: Charles Leclerc started first and finished second in 2023, Russell won from pole in 2024 and Norris was disqualified from second in 2025 after starting first. Verstappen won from second on the grid in 2023 and 2025. Recoveries are possible on the long straight: Sergio Pérez finished third from 11th in 2023, Hamilton second from tenth in 2024 and Antonelli third from 17th in 2025. That matters for Piastri, who has never finished on the podium there, and for McLaren, which has no podium at the circuit.

Temperature is the other variable. Track temperatures are expected at 10 to 12 degrees Celsius, RacingNews365 reports, and Verstappen called getting the tyres to work "the key" for Red Bull. Forecasts put the chance of rain in first practice above 50%, Motorsport.com reports; Norris said a wet session would be "scary" with the current intermediates.

## Economic impact

Formula 1 pays its prize fund to teams by constructors' position, so four of the five open fights carry money; the exact amounts per position are not published. The biggest swing is third, between Red Bull and McLaren, two teams with title-level budgets. Fifth would be the best result ever for the Faenza team, whose best finish under its Minardi, Toro Rosso, AlphaTauri and RB names is sixth, reached in 2008, 2019, 2021 and 2025. Seventh is decided between Haas and Audi, separated by three points.

Las Vegas is also F1's own commercial showcase. Stefano Domenicali has said the race generated more than $3.2 billion for the local economy across its three editions, The Race reported, which is why teams save special liveries and sponsor launches for this weekend. A penalty or a cold-tyre weekend there costs points in the same race where partners are paying the most for visibility.

## The framework

To estimate the open fights, we ran the same simulation we used for the drivers' fight for second last week. We took every team's actual points in each of the 20 Grands Prix of 2026, sprints excluded because none remain, picked three of those races at random to stand in for Las Vegas, Qatar and Abu Dhabi, added the points to the current table and repeated it 100,000 times.

Using all 20 races, Ferrari keeps second and Racing Bulls keeps fifth in practically every run. Red Bull keeps third in 81% of runs, McLaren takes it in 18% and 1% end level, which Red Bull would win on countback with three wins to McLaren's three and five second places to two. Williams keeps ninth in 99%. Haas keeps seventh in 63%, Audi passes it in 29% and 8% end level, which Haas would currently win on countback with a fifth place against Audi's best of eighth. Using only the last ten races, Red Bull's share rises to 89%, but Audi's form flips seventh: Audi passes Haas in 52% of runs and Haas holds it outright in 41%.

For the drivers, the same method gives Verstappen second in about 76% of runs on full-season form and 98% on the last ten races, with Russell at 13% and Hamilton at 8% on full-season form. Verstappen is also chasing a record: he has finished in the top three of the championship in seven straight seasons, from 2019 to 2025, level with Michael Schumacher and Stirling Moss, and one more would equal Hamilton's eight from 2014 to 2021. The model ignores track-specific strengths, penalties and reliability, so Piastri's grid drop and Antonelli's new turbo are not in it.

## Verdict

The data says most of these fights are settled in all but name. Ferrari can lock up second in Las Vegas unless Red Bull outscores it by nine points or more, and Racing Bulls' 45-point cushion over Alpine is safe on any recent form. The live ones are third, where McLaren needs a strong weekend that Piastri's penalty makes harder, and seventh, where three points and Audi's recent scoring make it a genuine contest.

My read: Las Vegas is the race where McLaren's season is decided. It has never finished on the podium there, it starts one car ten places back and it brings parts it calls the foundation of its 2027 car. If Red Bull leaves Nevada with its lead intact, third is effectively gone with two races to go.
