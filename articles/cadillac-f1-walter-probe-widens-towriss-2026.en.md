## What Happened

Since PaddockIntel's [September 18 report on the fraud suit against Cadillac F1 co-owner Mark Walter](/cadillac-f1-mark-walter-lawsuit-ownership-risk-2026), the federal scrutiny on Walter's businesses has widened on two fronts, and neither touches Cadillac F1 directly. First, Bloomberg reported that Dan Towriss — CEO of TWG Motorsports, which holds a stake in Cadillac F1 alongside General Motors — voluntarily traveled to New York in August to be interviewed by prosecutors investigating Walter's insurance companies and Guggenheim Partners. He was not questioned under oath and is not believed to be a target. Second, the SEC has joined the Department of Justice in seeking interviews with Guggenheim staff, per Bloomberg's September 17 report — a second federal agency now examining the same related-party disclosures at the center of the original lawsuit. Separately, UK Companies House records show Andretti Formula E Limited — a Walter/Towriss-linked entity tied to the group's Formula E history, not to Cadillac's F1 team — registered a new secured financing charge with HPS Investment Partners on August 11, still outstanding.

## Why It Happened

None of this is new pressure out of nowhere; it's the same investigation from the September 18 piece running its normal course. Federal prosecutors opened their inquiry into Guggenheim's asset-management arm in 2025, and the February 2026 grand jury subpoenas that forced Delaware Life to restate its related-party holdings from 3% to more than 42% were always going to produce follow-up interviews as the case develops. Towriss sits at the intersection of that probe and Cadillac F1 specifically because he runs TWG Motorsports, the entity built from the 2024 Andretti Global acquisition that now holds Cadillac F1's minority stake alongside GM. The SEC's parallel interest is the standard pattern when a DOJ criminal inquiry into disclosure practices surfaces: the securities regulator checks whether the same conduct also violated civil disclosure rules, which is a separate question from criminal fraud and does not imply either agency has reached a conclusion.

## Economic Impact

Nothing in either development changes Cadillac F1's on-track economics: the team's $215 million cost cap, its 2026 season, and General Motors' role as primary owner and financial backer are all untouched. The HPS financing charge is the more easily misread piece — a UK company registering a secured charge against its own assets is a routine part of raising debt, not evidence of distress, and Companies House filings confirm the charge sits against Andretti Formula E Limited, not against any Cadillac F1 asset. What's genuinely new is the shape of the exposure: a single-agency criminal probe has become a two-agency inquiry (DOJ and SEC), and TWG Motorsports' own CEO is now a named participant in it, even as a cooperating, non-target witness. That's a wider footprint on the same governance-opacity question PaddockIntel flagged in September — how much scrutiny an F1 team's shareholders face has no relationship to how much scrutiny the team's entry fee bought it.

## The Framework

The pattern from the original piece holds and sharpens: F1's $450 million anti-dilution entry fee (see [PaddockIntel's breakdown of how that fee works](/byd-geely-f1-entry-fee-anti-dilution-2026)) prices a grid slot, not the counterparty risk of the people financing it. A widening two-agency probe and a routine debt filing on a sister entity are, individually, unremarkable developments in a federal investigation. Together, they're a reminder that when an F1 team's ownership sits inside a diversified holding company, every headline about any other asset in that portfolio becomes a headline the team has to answer for — a dynamic PaddockIntel first flagged when [Renault used its Article 39 veto to control who could buy into Alpine](/alpine-f1-ownership-otro-capital-renault-veto-2026). Cadillac F1 didn't create this exposure and can't fully insulate itself from it; it inherited it the moment TWG Global became a shareholder.

## Verdict

Two agencies now instead of one, and TWG's own CEO now a cooperating witness instead of a bystander — that's more federal surface area on the Walter probe, not more risk to Cadillac F1's racing operation specifically. GM still runs the team, the cost cap is untouched, and nobody at Cadillac F1 has been questioned, named, or charged. The number to watch is still the one from September: whether TWG has to deny a sale a second time. It hasn't yet.
