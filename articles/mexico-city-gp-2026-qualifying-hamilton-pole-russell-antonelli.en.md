*Update, November 1, 2026: the stewards took no action over the slow-lap investigations, so Hamilton keeps pole, and disqualified Liam Lawson from qualifying for a technical breach. This analysis has been updated with the official starting grid.*

## What Happened

Lewis Hamilton took pole position for the Mexico City Grand Prix with 1:16.514, 0.009s ahead of George Russell and 0.119s ahead of his Ferrari teammate Charles Leclerc, according to Formula 1's timing. It is the 105th pole of his career, his first since the 2023 Hungarian Grand Prix, after 77 races without one, and his first as a Ferrari driver. Max Verstappen qualified fourth, 0.178s back, but his one-place penalty for Friday's clash with Liam Lawson drops him to fifth, so Kimi Antonelli, fifth fastest, starts fourth. Lando Norris, who topped Q2 and FP3, was sixth.

Two of Friday's and Saturday morning's front-runners fell away. Oscar Piastri went out in Q2 in 13th, 0.918s slower than Norris in the same session, RaceFans reports, after both McLarens used a single set of new softs. In Q1, Fernando Alonso's Aston Martin stopped at Turn 14 after losing water pressure and brought out a red flag. The stewards reprimanded him for leaving the car without replacing the steering wheel. Ryo Hirakawa, in Esteban Ocon's Haas, went out in Q1 in 19th, 0.875s slower than teammate Oliver Bearman.

## Why It Happened

Hamilton's pole was a lap without waste. The sum of his best three sectors from the whole session is 1:16.514, exactly his pole time, so his best first, second and third sectors all came on the same lap. He did not top any sector: Verstappen had the fastest first, Russell the fastest second and Leclerc the fastest third.

The drivers who beat him in a sector could not join theirs up. Verstappen's best sectors add up to 1:16.407, 0.107s under the pole time, but he locked up at Turn 13 on his final lap, RaceFans reports. Leclerc's add up to 1:16.443, Russell's to 1:16.512, 0.002s under Hamilton's. Antonelli, who had the best ideal lap of FP3, had the sixth best here, 1:16.654, and was the second fastest driver through the first sector.

## Economic Impact

A pole for Hamilton is a commercial moment as much as a sporting one. The seven-time champion joined Ferrari for 2025, and his first 42 races for the team, in PaddockIntel's database, produced no pole. SportsPro's 50 Most Marketable Athletes list ranks him 22nd in 2026, after first place in 2025, and his teammate Leclerc ninth. On Sunday both Ferraris start in the first two rows, Hamilton first and Leclerc third, after Leclerc topped both Friday sessions.

For Mercedes the constructors' arithmetic is unchanged by one session but sharper for it. In PaddockIntel's database, Mercedes leads Ferrari by 133 points and takes the title in Mexico if it outscores Ferrari by 39 this weekend. With Russell second and Antonelli fourth against Hamilton first and Leclerc third, the grid points to a close score between the two teams, not the 39-point swing Mercedes needs.

## The Framework

Mexico City has turned pole into victory half the time. In PaddockIntel's database, five of the 10 Grands Prix on the current layout between 2015 and 2025 were won from pole, and no winner started lower than third. The poles that did not convert went to Sebastian Vettel in 2017 (fourth), Daniel Ricciardo in 2018 (retired), Leclerc in 2019 (fourth), Valtteri Bottas in 2021 (15th) and Leclerc in 2023 (third). Hamilton has converted a Mexico pole before, in 2016.

The title math now runs through the front row. Antonelli has 365 points to Russell's 242 and Hamilton's 241, with 125 left. He needs two points, a ninth place, whatever they do. The only way he leaves Mexico without the title is if Russell or Hamilton wins and he finishes outside the top nine, or the top ten if the winner is Hamilton. On Sunday the two drivers who can stop him start first and second, and he starts fourth. He has finished ninth or better in 16 of 18 races this year.

The stewards confirmed the front of the grid. Hamilton, Gabriel Bortoleto, Bottas, Sergio Pérez and Isack Hadjar had all exceeded the maximum time between the safety car lines on slow laps, but the stewards took no further action because each had slowed to let other drivers pass. Liam Lawson, eighth fastest, was disqualified from qualifying because his MGU-K deployment did not step down as the technical rules require after the line on his best Q2 lap; he starts 22nd and last, behind Bottas, who drops 50 places for his power unit changes. Arvid Lindblad starts eighth and Pierre Gasly ninth.

## Verdict

The data says Hamilton earned this pole with the cleanest lap of the session, not the fastest car: Verstappen, Leclerc and Russell all had more pace in their sectors. My read: Sunday is the first race in weeks where Antonelli's title is genuinely in someone else's hands at the start, because the two drivers who can delay it are ahead of him. It still takes a win by one of them and a bad afternoon for Antonelli, who has had two in 18 races. With the front row confirmed, Hamilton's best chance of a first Ferrari win at this circuit is also the title's best chance of waiting another week.
