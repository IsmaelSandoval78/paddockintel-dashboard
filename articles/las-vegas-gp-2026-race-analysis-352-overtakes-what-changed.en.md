## What happened

Max Verstappen won the Las Vegas Grand Prix from pole, 0.639s ahead of Lando Norris, with Oscar Piastri third after starting 17th. Behind them came Kimi Antonelli, the Alpines of Pierre Gasly and Franco Colapinto, Lewis Hamilton, Carlos Sainz, George Russell and Charles Leclerc. It was Verstappen's 75th career win and, in PaddockIntel's database, his third in four editions of the race.

By Formula 1's own figures, reported by The Race, it was also the busiest race on record: 352 overtakes, 35 of them for the lead, against 17 in the whole 2025 edition; the 2023 Dutch Grand Prix, with 186, had been cited as the record. Our own analysis of the official timing feed, counting position changes at the timing points outside the safety car, found 16 occasions when a rival moved ahead of Verstappen: Russell nine times, Antonelli five, Isack Hadjar and Norris once each. Verstappen took the lead back every time and led about 45 of the 50 laps at the finish line.

The stewards' decisions after the race did not change the points. Hadjar received a 10-second penalty for slowing the field under the safety car before Red Bull's double stop, dropping from 14th to 15th, and Sergio Pérez a five-place grid penalty for Qatar for the collision with Rafael Câmara that caused it, Autosport reports. No penalty was announced for Piastri, who had been noted for a possible yellow flag infringement on the last lap.

## Why it happened

The cold, dense air and long straights made the slipstream unusually strong, and the 2026 power units turned energy into the deciding variable. Russell said he gained 1.3 seconds on the straight on Verstappen at times, Motorsport.com reports, but he could never hold the lead through the corners. Drivers saved energy to attack on the final lap, and that is where Antonelli's race ended: lifting to keep his overtake mode disrupted his battery harvesting, he reached the chicane with no energy and lost second and third to the McLarens, Crash.net reports.

Russell's own bid ended two laps earlier. Having dropped to third, he dived down the inside of Antonelli, locked up and hit the wall, a move he called high-risk, and finished ninth. At Ferrari, both drivers took 10-second penalties: Hamilton for passing Norris off the track on lap 13 and Leclerc for hitting Hamilton at Turn 7 on lap 38. Team principal Fred Vasseur called Leclerc in after the race, RacingNews365 reports.

## Economic impact

Formula 1 pays its prize fund by constructors' position, so the race moved money as well as points; the exact amounts per position are not published. McLaren's double podium cut Red Bull's lead for third from 17 points to nine. "It's a hell of a lot of money from P4 to P3," Norris told RaceFans before the weekend. Ferrari scored seven points to Red Bull's 25, so its lead for second fell from 95 to 77 and it leaves Las Vegas without that place secured.

Lower down, Alpine scored 18 points with its best result of the year, Gasly's fifth, taking it to 87, 27 behind Racing Bulls in the fight for fifth. Williams moved nine points clear of Aston Martin for ninth with Sainz's eighth place from 20th on the grid. Haas and Audi both failed to score, so Haas keeps its three-point lead for seventh. Every figure here matches RaceFans' championship table.

## The framework

We reran our simulation for the last two races. Qatar and Abu Dhabi are each drawn at random from the 21 Grands Prix of 2026, Las Vegas included, using every team's and driver's actual points in that race, and the process is repeated 100,000 times. It is the same method as before the weekend, now with the real Las Vegas result instead of a grid-based estimate.

The results: Verstappen keeps second in more than 99% of runs; he clinches it in Qatar unless Hamilton outscores him there by 13 points or more, or Russell or Norris by 16 or more; in a tie on points he would win on countback. The fight for third is open: Hamilton takes it in about 42% of runs, Norris in 30% and Russell in 27%, with about 3% ending level. Red Bull keeps third in the constructors' table in about 75% of runs, down from 93% before the race, and McLaren takes it in 24%. Haas keeps seventh in about 67%, Audi takes it in 26% and 8% end level. Ferrari keeps second in practically every run and clinches it in Qatar unless Red Bull outscores it there by 34 points or more.

The record should be read with care. Overtake counts include passes that the energy rules made temporary, and our 16 lead changes are counted at the timing points, so a pass on a straight and the re-pass under braking both register. Counting at the finish line, the lead changed hands only a handful of times. The model ignores track-specific pace: Qatar will be decided on tyres, with Pirelli limiting stints to 27 laps.

## Verdict

The data says Las Vegas settled one fight and opened two. Second in the drivers' championship is effectively Verstappen's, while third among the drivers is now a three-way contest and third in the constructors' table is down to nine points. Ferrari's second place, which it could have clinched in Las Vegas, now waits for Qatar.

My read: the record is less a sign of great racing than of rules that made passing cheap and leading expensive. Verstappen won by understanding that better than anyone, staying in front at the two points of the lap that mattered. The V8 era agreed this week is meant to end this kind of race; on this evidence, many drivers will welcome the trade.
