## What Happened

Kimi Antonelli is the 2026 Formula 1 world champion. He finished third in the Mexico City Grand Prix, behind Max Verstappen and Lewis Hamilton, and at 20 years and 68 days he is the youngest champion in the sport's history: Sebastian Vettel was 23 years and 134 days old when he won the 2010 title. Antonelli is the first Italian champion since Alberto Ascari in 1953, and he has won it with four races to spare.

Verstappen won by 0.799s from Hamilton, according to Formula 1's timing, his sixth win at the Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez. No other driver has won the Mexican Grand Prix more than twice. Charles Leclerc was fourth, Lando Norris fifth and George Russell sixth.

The race had three parts. On lap one Leclerc and pole sitter Hamilton both ran off at Turn 4, and Leclerc rejoined in the lead; the stewards gave him five seconds. Antonelli, who made his only scheduled stop later than the leaders, led laps 28 to 43. One lap after he pitted, Valtteri Bottas crashed at Turn 1 after a front-left suspension failure, and the race was stopped for 40 minutes. Verstappen led the restart from a standing start and held off Hamilton to the flag.

## Why It Happened

The red flag decided the order at the front. Antonelli pitted on lap 43 and rejoined fourth; on the next lap Bottas crashed, the safety car came out and the race was suspended on lap 45, so every driver who had not stopped got a free tyre change in the pit lane. Had Antonelli stayed out one more lap he would have restarted in the lead, RacingNews365 notes. Instead the field lined up Verstappen, Leclerc, Hamilton, Antonelli, all on soft tyres, for a 26-lap sprint.

Verstappen's start on the medium tyre gave him the strategy that the stoppage rewarded. He made his first stop on lap 27, came out ahead of Leclerc once Leclerc served his penalty, and inherited the lead when Antonelli stopped. From there his record at this circuit did the rest: in PaddockIntel's database, he has led at the end of lap one in six of his ten races here, and he led the restart into Turn 1 again.

Leclerc's penalty was the race's argument. The stewards said both Ferraris arrived at Turn 4 too fast to make the corner, so Leclerc was not entitled to it, and that he completed the pass off track. They cut the usual ten seconds to five because Hamilton was also off track and it was the opening lap. "The stewards are basically handing Charles this win," Hamilton said on the radio, Crash.net reports. Russell got ten seconds for causing a collision with Isack Hadjar at Turn 6.

## Economic Impact

A title changes what a driver is worth. F1 Salaries puts Antonelli's 2026 base salary at Mercedes at about $2 million, with performance bonuses on top; a world title is the strongest negotiating position a driver can bring to his next deal. For Mercedes, it is the first drivers' title since Hamilton's in 2020 and the return on a driver it signed as a junior.

The constructors' fight is not over, and it has prize money attached. On the FIA's final classification, Mercedes has 630 points to Ferrari's 504: a lead of 126 with 129 left in the last three races. Mercedes needed to outscore Ferrari by 39 points in Mexico to clinch the title here and instead was outscored by seven, so the earliest it can now win is Brazil, if it outscores Ferrari by at least four points there. Behind them, Red Bull moved back ahead of McLaren in the fight for third, 382 points to 365, after the stewards gave Oscar Piastri a ten-second penalty after the race that dropped him out of the points.

## The Framework

Antonelli's season reads like a champion's at any age. In PaddockIntel's database, he has nine wins and 16 podiums from 19 races in 2026, and his title was settled with 100 points still available. He leads Verstappen, now second, by 119 points, and Hamilton by 121.

Mexico also confirmed the circuit's pattern. Verstappen started fifth after his one-place penalty, lower than any winner on the current layout before today, but the red flag reset the grid: he restarted from first. On a circuit where no winner had started lower than third since 2015, the race was decided by who led after the stoppage, not by the original grid.

## Verdict

The data says Antonelli won this title long before Mexico: nine wins and only two finishes outside the top nine all year left his rivals needing a miracle, and none came. My read: the more telling number from Sunday is the 16 laps he led on the day he only needed ninth place. A driver managing a title would have stayed behind; Antonelli ran a long first stint to win the race, and only a crash one lap after his stop took that chance away.
