## What Happened

Kimi Antonelli arrives at the Mexico City Grand Prix on Sunday needing two points to win the 2026 championship. After Austin he has 365 points; George Russell has 242 and Lewis Hamilton 241, and with five races and no sprints left, 125 points remain. A ninth place on November 1 settles it whatever the others do. In PaddockIntel's count, even a tie on points at the end of the year would go his way: Russell, with three wins, could reach eight at most and Hamilton, with one, six; Antonelli already has nine.

He can also be champion with less, as RacingNews365 and Automoto.it point out. If neither Russell nor Hamilton wins the race, Antonelli is champion even without scoring, because second place, 18 points, would still leave both of them more than 100 points behind with 100 left. If Hamilton wins, tenth is enough. Only a win for Russell or Hamilton, with Antonelli outside the points positions that matter, moves the date again. At 20 years and 68 days, he would become Formula 1's youngest world champion, more than three years younger than Sebastian Vettel was in 2010, at 23 years and 134 days.

## Why It Happened

The margin rests on how rarely Antonelli has had a bad Sunday. In PaddockIntel's database he has finished ninth or better in 16 of the 18 Grands Prix of 2026, and he has been classified in all of them. The two exceptions came in June and July: 16th in Barcelona after retiring late enough to be classified and 15th at Silverstone from pole. He has nine wins and seven poles.

His rivals have not kept that rhythm. Russell has scored six points in the last three rounds, counting the Singapore sprint, the same as Nico Hülkenberg, 14th in the standings, as RaceFans first noted; Toto Wolff said in Austin that Russell "has it all, but he's not clicking with the car". Hamilton is the one in form: third, sixth plus second in the sprint, and fourth in the last three rounds, 42 points. But the scenario that stops Antonelli needs a win, and Hamilton has won once this year, in Barcelona.

## Economic Impact

What the title is worth to Antonelli is not public. His Mercedes contract has never been disclosed; reported estimates put his 2026 base salary between $2 million and £2.6 million, with performance bonuses of up to $7.5 million (QuiFinanza) or $12 million (F1 Salaries), and none of those sources gives a figure for the title itself. On the team's side, Wolff said this week that Mercedes has spent "more than 10 million" on Antonelli's development since signing him for its junior programme, F1Mania reports, without giving a currency.

The clearer number is the audience. Austin drew Sky Italia's best Formula 1 audience since 2022, an average of 1,917,000 viewers and a 9.8% share, according to a Sky statement reported by Automoto.it. Mexico City starts at 9 p.m. in Italy, the same slot, with an Italian driver one result from the title. Audiences like that are what broadcast rights end up priced on.

The constructors' championship can be settled in Mexico too. Mercedes leads Ferrari by 133 points, 607 to 474. With four races left after Mexico, a team can score at most 172, so Mercedes needs to outscore Ferrari by 39 points this weekend, a one-two with Ferrari on four points or fewer, for example.

## The Framework

Pole matters less in Mexico City than the title arithmetic might suggest. In PaddockIntel's database, the pole sitter has won 12 of the 25 Grands Prix at the Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez, and only 5 of the 10 since the race returned in 2015; three of the other five winners started third. Saturday's grid will shape the race, but it has not decided it here half the time.

The rest of the field brings its own stakes. Ferrari has had a driver on the podium in six of its last ten Mexico City races but has won only once, Carlos Sainz from pole in 2024, and Charles Leclerc said after Austin that the team is "not robust enough" over a weekend. Sergio Pérez starts his 300th Grand Prix at home, where his two podiums, in 2021 and 2022, are the only ones a Mexican driver has taken at his home race, though his Cadillac has no points in 18 races.

## Verdict

My read: the title is decided in all but the paperwork. Antonelli needs a ninth place he has achieved 16 times in 18 races, and the only scenario that delays him needs a win from a driver who has won once this year or from one who has scored six points in three rounds. Automoto.it writes that he is targeting the win rather than the two points; if so, the more open question is whether pole will be worth as much here as it was in Austin.
