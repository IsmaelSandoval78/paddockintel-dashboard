## What Happened

Lando Norris was fastest in the final practice session of the Mexico City Grand Prix with 1:17.600, 0.001s ahead of Kimi Antonelli and 0.117s ahead of Charles Leclerc, according to the FIA classification. Oscar Piastri was fourth, 0.195s back, and Max Verstappen fifth, 0.351s back. Lewis Hamilton was sixth and George Russell seventh, half a second off. All of the top times came on soft tyres in the last quarter of the hour, the qualifying simulations that make FP3 the best guide to Saturday afternoon.

The session was quicker than Friday's. Norris's lap was 0.227s faster than Leclerc's 1:17.827, the best time of FP2, with the track temperature at up to 48.8 °C against FP2's 38.8 °C. There were no red flags, and 17 of 405 timed laps were deleted for track limits.

Two cars barely ran. Gabriel Bortoleto did two laps and Sergio Pérez one, both without a time, while Audi and Cadillac changed their power units, PlanetF1 reported. In the Haas, Ryo Hirakawa did 32 laps, as many as anyone, after the team stood down Esteban Ocon on Saturday morning. Hirakawa was 19th, 0.577s slower than his teammate Oliver Bearman.

## Why It Happened

The single fastest lap hides where the time was. PaddockIntel added up each driver's best three sectors from every valid lap, using FastF1, which reads Formula 1's official timing feed. **Antonelli's best sectors come to 1:17.422, 0.098s under Norris's 1:17.520 and 0.199s under Leclerc's 1:17.621.** Antonelli was 0.130s quicker than anyone in the second sector, the twisty middle of the lap, with 30.436 against Norris's 30.566. Leclerc had the best first sector, which includes the long main straight, and the best third, through the stadium section. Norris did not top any sector, but he put his three together on one lap better than the others.

An ideal lap is a ceiling, not a lap anyone drove. Antonelli's best first and third sectors came on a lap whose middle sector was 0.199s slower than his best one, so his margin depends on putting the pieces together under qualifying pressure. The gap between his ideal and his best lap, 0.179s, was the largest of the top three. Norris left 0.080s on the table and Leclerc 0.096s.

## Economic Impact

The weekend's other story is a seat. Haas asked the stewards to replace Ocon with Hirakawa "for the rest of the competition", and they approved it with Hirakawa using Ocon's engine and tyre allocation, according to the stewards' decision. The team gave no reason beyond saying Ocon was not ill. Team principal Ayao Komatsu, asked on Sky Sports F1, said: "I can't tell you much, but at least I can tell you that he's not ill. That's as much I can tell you." Speedcafe reports that Ocon had argued on the radio in FP2 over being sent out on mediums rather than softs, and that there is no confirmation the exchange played any part.

The points table shows what Haas is risking. In PaddockIntel's database, Haas is seventh in the constructors' championship with 30 points, seven ahead of Audi, with five races left. Ocon scored seven of those 30 points and Bearman 23. Each place in the constructors' table is worth more in the prize-money split than the one below it, so Haas is changing drivers in the middle of a close fight, with a 32-year-old making his Grand Prix debut. Hirakawa races for Toyota in the World Endurance Championship at Monza next weekend, so he is not available for Brazil, and Komatsu would not say who drives there. Speedcafe reports that Ferrari junior Rafael Câmara has already been confirmed in Ocon's seat for 2027.

Elsewhere, the stewards gave Isack Hadjar a formal warning after he pushed aside a paddock guest who had approached him "in an aggressive manner" before FP1. They counted the guest's uninvited approach in mitigation, so Hadjar starts without a penalty, unlike his teammate Verstappen, who drops one grid place for Friday's clash with Liam Lawson.

## The Framework

Mexico City makes qualifying count. In PaddockIntel's database, five of the 10 Grands Prix held on the current layout between 2015 and 2025 were won from pole, and no winner started lower than third. Norris won here from pole last year. That raises the stakes on a session where the top three are covered by 0.199s on ideal laps, and where Verstappen's penalty means that third in qualifying puts him fourth on the grid, a slot no Mexico winner on this layout has started from.

The title does not depend on any of that. Antonelli has 365 points to Russell's 242 and Hamilton's 241, with 125 left. He needs two points, a ninth place, whatever they do. If neither Russell nor Hamilton wins on Sunday, he is champion even without scoring; if Hamilton wins, tenth is enough. Russell was seventh in FP3, half a second off, and Hamilton sixth after his Friday power failure. Neither looked like a driver about to take pole.

## Verdict

The data says the pole fight is between three drivers covered by two tenths, and that Antonelli has the most in hand. Norris put the best lap together and Leclerc owns both ends of the circuit, but Antonelli's second sector is the biggest single advantage anyone showed on Saturday morning. My read: Antonelli is the favourite for pole if he joins up his sectors. On a circuit where the winner has always started in the top three, a pole would turn a title he needs only ninth place for into a chance to clinch it with a win.
