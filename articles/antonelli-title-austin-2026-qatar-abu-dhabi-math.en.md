## What happened

Kimi Antonelli left Singapore on 340 points, 102 clear of his Mercedes teammate George Russell (238). Lewis Hamilton is 111 behind on 229, Max Verstappen 119 behind on 221, Charles Leclerc 125 behind on 215 and Lando Norris 139 behind on 201. Six Grands Prix remain and none of them has a Sprint, so 150 points are still available. The next one is the United States Grand Prix in Austin on October 25.

How many of those six races actually happen is still open. On October 3, F1 CEO Stefano Domenicali told Sky Sport Italia that "the calendar is confirmed: the last two grands prix will be in Qatar and Abu Dhabi," on November 29 and December 6. On October 12, Motorsport.com reported that F1 was moving closer to activating its contingency plan, a single finale at Imola on December 6, after a series of attacks on Riyadh's international airport. Neither Middle East race has been cancelled, and teams were told to expect a final call by mid-October.

## Why it happened

The title math is simple once the calendar is fixed. Antonelli is champion when his lead is larger than the points left. A tie on points goes to wins, where he has eight to Russell's three, and in every Austin scenario below that tiebreak falls his way, so a lead equal to the points left is also enough.

On the official calendar, 125 points remain after Austin. Antonelli would need to outscore Russell by 23 points there, Hamilton by 14 and Verstappen by 6. Formula1.com's data team reached the same figures. In race results, that means only one combination works: Antonelli wins, Russell finishes ninth or lower and Hamilton fifth or lower. Leclerc and Norris can't stop an Austin win on their own.

Russell finishing ninth or lower is less likely than it looked a week ago. He started 19th in Singapore after an engine change and finished ninth. He told Sky Sports he is "maybe looking at Vegas" for his next power-unit penalty, according to GPblog, so he should start Austin from his qualifying position. Mercedes also expects to run its newer aerodynamic package on both cars there.

Under the contingency, the numbers change. One Imola race in place of Qatar and Abu Dhabi leaves 125 points after Singapore and 100 after Austin. By our own calculation from the standings, Antonelli would then clinch in Austin unless Russell outscores him there by three points or more, Hamilton by 12 or more, or Verstappen by 20 or more.

## Economic impact

The calendar call is a money decision before it is a sporting one. As we explained in September, [F1's hosting contracts put the cost of a cancellation on whoever cancels](/f1-2026-qatar-abu-dhabi-hosting-fee-standoff/). A promoter that withdraws still owes Formula One Management its fee. If FOM pulls the race itself, FOM forfeits the fee. Neither fee is published, but reported estimates put Qatar's at around $55 million a year and Abu Dhabi's at around $42 million, close to $100 million combined.

That is why the official line has held. Domenicali's October 3 statement keeps both fees on F1's books. The Imola option has a cost of its own: F1 authorised temporary grandstands and other preparatory work there, and has said it will pay for it even if the work goes unused.

For Antonelli, the title has a contract value. Mercedes' public announcement covered only his 2026 seat. His reported base salary is $2 million, a figure Mercedes has never confirmed, and F1 Salaries estimates his bonus potential at up to $12 million this season. [Our breakdown of his contract](/antonelli-salary-2026/) shows how far that sits from the reported €92 million a year Red Bull committed to keep Verstappen. A world title is the strongest argument a driver can bring to the next negotiation.

## The framework

Antonelli is not chasing an ordinary title. He will be 20 years and 61 days old in Austin. Sebastian Vettel, the youngest champion so far, was 23 years and 134 days old when he won the 2010 title at the season's last race. Antonelli would beat that mark by more than three years whenever he clinches this season. He would also be Italy's first drivers' champion since Alberto Ascari in 1953, and only its third ever after Nino Farina in 1950 and Ascari.

The season's numbers support the lead. Antonelli has averaged 20.0 points per race over 17 rounds; Russell has averaged 14.0. That rate of scoring is what lets the calendar, not Russell, decide where the title is settled. As we argued after [his record pole in Shanghai](/antonelli-youngest-pole-mercedes-commercial-asset-2026/), age-based records are a commercial asset for Mercedes in their own right, and a youngest-ever title would be the largest of them.

## Verdict

The data points to Mexico City or later on the official calendar. An Austin title needs a win plus two specific results from other drivers, and Russell's deferred engine penalty removes the easiest route to one of them.

My read: the real decider is F1's contract math, not Russell. If F1 keeps Qatar and Abu Dhabi, it keeps up to $100 million in fees and Austin becomes a long shot. If it triggers Imola, Austin becomes close to a formality.
