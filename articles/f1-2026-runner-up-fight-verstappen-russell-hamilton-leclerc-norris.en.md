## What happened

The title is settled, but the race behind it is not. Kimi Antonelli clinched the 2026 championship in Mexico and leaves Brazil on 390 points. Behind him, five drivers are separated by 33 points: Max Verstappen on 279, George Russell on 262, Lewis Hamilton on 261, Charles Leclerc on 249 and Lando Norris on 246, in the FIA's standings after the São Paulo Grand Prix. Three Grands Prix remain, Las Vegas on November 22, Qatar on November 29 and Abu Dhabi on December 6, none of them with a sprint, so each driver can score at most 75 more points.

The order has turned over in five races. After the Azerbaijan Grand Prix in round 15, Russell was second, 73 points clear of Verstappen. Since then, Verstappen has scored 116 points to Russell's 26 in our database, sprint included: wins in the Bahrain Grand Prix held in Malaysia, in Singapore and in Mexico, third in Austin and second from 21st on the grid in Brazil. Russell's best finish in those five races was fourth, in Brazil. Motorsport.com lists the reasons: a power unit failure in Malaysia, a sprint crash and an engine penalty in Singapore, a poor race in Austin, a deployment issue in Mexico and a spin at the pit exit in São Paulo. Mercedes has said it may now back him: "We absolutely want him to score the maximum and hopefully finish second," Toto Wolff told Crash.net after Brazil, adding that the team would "go full blast".

## Why it happened

Second place is Verstappen's to lose, and the arithmetic shows how much. He cannot secure it in Las Vegas: even a win there with Russell scoring nothing would leave him 42 points ahead with 50 still available. The earliest he can lock it in is Qatar, with a lead of more than 25 points after that race.

To pass him, each rival needs a points swing over three races. Russell has to outscore Verstappen by 18, or by 17 with a better countback; Hamilton by 19; Leclerc by 31; Norris by 34. Countback matters here because the first tiebreak is wins, and Verstappen, Russell and Norris all have three this season, while Verstappen has five second places to Russell's four. In practice Russell needs about six points a race more than Verstappen, roughly finishing second every weekend while Verstappen finishes fourth, which is the opposite of the last five rounds.

The tracks lean the same way. At the Las Vegas Strip circuit, Verstappen has two wins from three starts, in 2023 and 2025, and Russell won in 2024. At Lusail, Verstappen has won three of the four Qatar Grands Prix and finished second in the other; Russell's best there is fourth, and Hamilton has finished 12th in each of the last two. At Yas Marina, Verstappen and Hamilton have five wins each, but Verstappen's came in 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023 and 2025. Across the three venues, Verstappen has ten wins from 18 starts; the other four drivers have eight between them, five of them Hamilton's in Abu Dhabi.

## Economic impact

Formula 1 pays its prize fund to teams by constructors' position, not to drivers for theirs, so finishing second in the drivers' standings pays nothing directly. What it decides is reputation and leverage. For Mercedes, a Russell second would complete its first one-two in the drivers' standings since 2020, when Lewis Hamilton and Valtteri Bottas finished first and second. For Verstappen, it would be a second consecutive runner-up finish after losing the 2025 title to Norris by two points, and the strongest evidence for his own line that he would quit only if he stopped believing he was the best.

The same points also feed the constructors' table, where the money is. Ferrari, with Hamilton and Leclerc, leads Red Bull by 95 points for second with 129 left. Red Bull leads McLaren, Norris's team, by 17 for third. Every point Verstappen takes for himself is also a point toward Red Bull catching Ferrari and holding off McLaren, and every point Hamilton and Leclerc score protects a Ferrari position that has gone from a 122-point lead to 95 in one race.

## The framework

To put a number on the chances, we ran a simple simulation. We took the actual finishing points of every driver in each of the 20 Grands Prix of 2026, picked three of those races at random to stand in for Las Vegas, Qatar and Abu Dhabi, added the points to the current standings, and repeated it 100,000 times, with ties settled on countback. Drawing whole races keeps the results consistent: two drivers can't finish in the same place.

Using all 20 races, Verstappen finishes second in about 75% of runs, Russell in 15%, Hamilton in 8% and Leclerc and Norris in about 1% each. Using only the last ten races, the second half of the season, when Red Bull's Ford-built power unit started winning, Verstappen's share rises to about 95%, Norris's is about 3% and Russell's about 2%. The model has clear limits: it ignores track-specific strengths, team orders and reliability changes, and it treats each race as independent. It is a measure of form, not a forecast.

## Verdict

The data points to Verstappen. A 17-point lead is not decisive on its own, but combined with the last five rounds, his record at the three remaining circuits and a simulation that gives him second in three runs out of four even on full-season form, it is the clear favourite position.

My read: the fight for second is really a fight for Russell's reputation inside Mercedes. Wolff's offer to go "full blast" can buy strategy priority, but the only teammate who can hand Russell points is Antonelli, and he is champion already. If Russell is going to finish second, it has to start in Las Vegas, the one remaining track where he has won.
