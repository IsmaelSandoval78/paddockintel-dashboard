Craig Scarborough spotting new front wishbone fairings shaping Aston Martin's aero
rake at Monza. The rake itself is the sensor array — rows of pressure, temperature,
and flow-direction probes — teams bolt onto a car in practice to map real airflow
around the front end before committing to a final aero package, rather than trusting
only wind-tunnel and CFD numbers. Fairings on the rake's own mounting structure are a
subtler tell than the data it collects: they show the team is now also managing the
rake's aerodynamic footprint so it disturbs the flow it's measuring less. Scarborough
flags it without overclaiming — he's explicit he isn't sure if it points to a genuine
new front-end concept for Aston Martin or just more refined baseline data-gathering.
That "watch, don't assume" read is exactly the technical literacy PaddockIntel wants
readers to build around car-development stories.
