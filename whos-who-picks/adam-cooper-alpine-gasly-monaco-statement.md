Adam Cooper posting Alpine's official statement after the FIA's International Court of
Appeal reinstated Pierre Gasly's two pitlane-speeding penalties from the 2026 Monaco GP,
dropping him from P3 to P7 and handing Isack Hadjar the podium he'd already celebrated.
The case's real substance, confirmed independently: Gasly's penalties were first rescinded
on a Right of Review after Formula One Management admitted a measurement error in the
pitlane distance used to calculate his speed — then McLaren and Red Bull appealed that
reversal, arguing it retroactively changed the benchmark teams had calibrated their pit
limiters against mid-season, which would undermine sporting fairness. The Court sided
with McLaren/Red Bull on Friday. Alpine's statement — "disappointment and frustration,"
still maintaining Gasly did nothing wrong — is the team's on-record response to a
decision that turns on a genuinely technical stewarding question (whose measurement
error counts, and when a fix becomes unfair to rivals who trusted the original number),
not a simple driving infraction.
